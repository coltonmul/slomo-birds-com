The Footage
Levels.

Every stretch of slow motion bird footage gets graded 0 through 5. The grade decides where it goes: the daily clip, the films, the ambient mixes, or the archive. An automated pass assigns the first grade from how much is actually happening in frame; the final call is always human, and every human correction teaches the system this yard's version of interesting.

5banger

The 60-second clip you send people

Birds fighting mid-air. Hummingbirds doing hummingbird things. Multiple species on screen negotiating who owns the feeder. Peak motion, peak drama, no dead frames.

Feeds: daily social clip · film openers
4strong

Whole sequences that hold attention

Sustained interesting action: arrivals and takeoffs stacking up, active squabbles, several distinct moments per minute. Not quite a banger, never boring.

Feeds: the films · strong cuts
3the cut

Moving and going

Birds relocating, feeding flurries, real motion without aggression. The workhorse tier: most of what makes a watchable long-form film lives here.

Feeds: standard selects
2ambient

The background layer

Minimal activity, but deliberately kept. This is the connective tissue of a ten-hour ambient mix: mostly calm, so that when something moves in your periphery, it feels like you caught it yourself.

Feeds: ambient mixes · long-form TV loops
1logged

Bird present, nothing happening

A finch parked at a port, pecking, for a long stretch. On record, searchable, and almost never used.

Feeds: nothing by default · kept in the log
0dead

The camera was just running

Empty frame, swaying branches, no visitors. Flagged so nobody ever reviews it again.

Feeds: nothing · flagged dead

How grading works

A motion-analysis pass watches every frame and scores what the eye would: how much movement, how fast, how many birds, arrivals and exits, wing bursts versus idle pecking. Levels 0 through 3 come from that signal directly. The 4/5 line involves judgment (a fight and a feeding flurry can carry similar energy), so the top tiers start as educated guesses and get corrected in review.

The correction is the clever part: in the edit, each graded clip sits on the video track matching its level. Regrading a clip means dragging it up or down a track, which takes one second, and the system reads those corrections back and adjusts its thresholds for that camera setup. Reviewing the footage is training it.

Known edge case under calibration: the rotating feeder, whose spin reads as constant motion. Its zone gets its own raised baseline so the spin itself never inflates a grade.
