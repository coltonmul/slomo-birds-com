Privacy.

The Slow Motion Birds apps do not collect any data about you. There is nothing to opt out of, because there is nothing being gathered.

v1.5.1 · Updated Sat / 2026-10-03 / 7:02 PM CT

No accounts. No sign in. No analytics. No advertising and no advertising identifiers. No tracking, and no data shared with anyone. That is true of every version, and it is the part that will not change.

Two versions, and what is different about them

Version 1.0, the one on the App Store, makes no network requests at all. Every clip is bundled inside the app itself, so once it is installed it works with the Apple TV disconnected from the internet.

Version 1.1, which adds a clip of the day, does reach the internet, and this page says so before that version ships rather than after. It reads three things and sends nothing about you:

  • A small published file that says which clip is the clip of the day and the clip of the week, and the video for those clips. Both are ordinary downloads of public files, the same as a web page fetching an image.
  • If, and only if, you type a town or a ZIP code into the app, it asks our own small service how many places nearby have reported that bird to eBird lately. That service holds the eBird key, answers from its own cache, and does not store or log what you typed, what it resolved to, or that you asked at all.

The app never asks for your location. There is no location permission in it, and no code in it that reads one. The only way it knows anything about where you are is the town or ZIP you choose to type, and that stays on your own device.

Fourteen clips are still built into every version, so the app still plays with no internet at all.

What stays on your device

These things, and they never leave it: whether shuffle is on or off, which clips you starred as favorites, whether you chose to play favorites only, whether the app opens each session with the clip of the day, whether the nerdy camera details are shown, the town or ZIP you typed for bird counts, and a list of the past clips of the day the app has shown you. Your Apple TV, iPhone or iPad stores those the same way it stores any other app setting. Deleting the app removes them, and version 1.1 also has a button in its settings that forgets all of them at once.

The clips the app has downloaded are kept in its cache so it keeps working offline for a week. They are just video files, they say nothing about you, and the system may clear them whenever it needs the space.

The footage

Every clip in the app was shot by Colton Mulligan, in his own backyard in Nashville and on his own trips. No cameras in the app, no photos read from your device, no access to anything on your Apple TV, iPhone or iPad.

Where the bird counts come from

Nearby sighting numbers come from eBird, run by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, and the app says so wherever it shows them. The app talks to our own service rather than to eBird directly, so that eBird never sees you, only us, and all we ever send is a rounded map location for the place you typed and the name of the bird on screen.

Children

The app is rated 4 and up. Since it collects nothing from anyone, it collects nothing from children either.

Changes

If a future version of the app ever does collect something, this page changes first and the version stamp above moves with it. That is the rule, and version 1.1 is the first time it has been used: this page described 1.1's network reads before 1.1 was submitted to Apple.

About this website

Everything above is about the app. This part is about the website you are reading it on, slowmotionbirds.com, which is a separate thing from the app.

The website counts page views with a cookie-free visitor counter (Cloudflare Web Analytics). It sets no cookies, does not fingerprint anyone, and counts visits, not people. That counter is deliberately left off this one page. Like the rest of the site, this page does load its typefaces from Google Fonts, so your browser contacts Google to fetch them. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, and the films on its other pages are embedded from Vimeo.

If you sign up for "keep me posted" on the homepage, that's the one time this website collects anything: your email address, what you checked (if anything), and when you signed up. It's used only to send you the updates you asked for, it's never sold or shared, and you can unsubscribe anytime. The list is shared with betterbirder.com, which uses the same signup form and the same rule.

The full plain-language notice for this website and Colton's other sites, covering what is counted, what is never done and how to reach him, is at coltonland.com/privacy.
